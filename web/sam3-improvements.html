SAM3 follow-up

October 24, 2026 · NBHL ball hockey

The selected SAM3 fine-tune scored 88.1% detection F1, compared with 52.8% for pretrained SAM3, on 36 reused regression frames. Adding the automatically predicted rink surface raised F1 to 90.8%, versus 89.0% for the prior YOLO detector.

The existing SAM2 + OSNet + Qwen pipeline remains the recommended tracking baseline. Adding SAM3 agreement retained 111/129 correct connections and zero known wrong merges, while reducing unverified joins from four to three. The fine-tuned SAM3 video tracker also recovered 111 connections, but made three wrong merges. These are sparse, reused checks; the change in unverified joins is not a verified accuracy gain.

Audit report

How the fine-tuning was done

196 complete reviewed frames from three NBHL recordings, with horizontal flips (392 training examples). Validation: 23 frames from a fourth recording. Six weight-training configurations were tried. The selected checkpoint updates the grounding transformer and scoring layers; text, the visual backbone, masks and temporal tracking weights stay frozen. The separate visual-backbone experiment also trained the final four attention blocks.

AdamW, Hungarian box assignment, L1 and generalized-IoU box losses, and focal classification loss. The selected run used learning rate 0.00001 and epoch 2. Only validation chose the epoch and confidence threshold. Gradient checks, changed-weight counts and hashes of every frozen parameter verify that training happened and frozen weights stayed fixed.

What was combined and how settings were selected

Detector anchoring assigns SAM3 masks to the current detector’s boxes with one-to-one overlap matching. Appearance checks split a fragment when several clear observations disagree. Consensus variants use SAM3 agreement when reconnecting SAM2 fragments, with or without Qwen image embeddings. Native experiments change association, mask reconditioning and track lifetime. Visual-prompt experiments use an automatically selected initial player box; the persistent variant keeps its encoded visual template available on later frames.

Selection maximizes correct − 10 × wrong, then observation coverage, then fewer unverified joins. Each family keeps one development winner. All tested alternatives remain in the linked evidence.

Long replay and identity audit

The 20m 15s replay covers recording 29:45–50:00. It uses four 315-second executions with 15-second overlaps. Shared frames were checked byte-for-byte. IDs are reconciled only when simultaneous overlapping boxes agree; gaps remain gaps. The displayed labels are automatic fragments, not jersey numbers, and masks can drift or persist behind occlusion.

The method selector also offers the existing SAM2 runs with generic and trained appearance for the complete interval. Those runs are newly exported, without retraining or manual correction. Full-game identity accuracy remains unverified for all three methods.

A new 42:00–42:30 interval was labeled before looking at either tracker’s IDs. Four initially visible players were checked once per second, giving 88 visible observations; 29 out-of-view and seven uncertain observations were excluded. Both trackers had the same 135 seconds of preceding context. The recording was reused and boxes were assisted by detector proposals.
