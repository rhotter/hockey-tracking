What was trained and how this was tested

Twelve appearance-training runs compared whole-player and three-body-region OSNet features, DINOv2, frozen-backbone heads, later-layer training, stronger preservation of generic features, different seeds, same-team batches and a small set of reviewed temporal examples. The larger reviewed identity set contains 211 usable crops of 34 local identities from four NBHL recordings. Temporal experiments added 24 weakly labeled crops; those are correlated training examples, not independent ground truth.

Training used supervised contrastive and pair losses. This fine-tunes the appearance component used to reconnect fragments; SAM2 weights and the motion trackers were not trained end to end. Both DINOv2 runs selected the initial checkpoint, so they did not demonstrate a fine-tuning improvement.

Development covered 9 clips and 320 seconds from two other recordings. Settings were selected on those checks. Global assignment of fragment links produced the largest development gain. Training did not establish a clear advantage over the strongest generic-feature control. The tables above test transfer to the new recordings.

The two new sources are the Pittsburgh Legends semifinal and Maryland women’s Tier 2 final. Their sampling times were reserved before inspection, candidate code and checkpoint hashes were frozen before reference review, and the reference was sealed before inference. The assistant reviewed 233 observations in 39 reserved frames with raw temporal context. Pairs share observations and are correlated. There is no independent human annotation, whole-video IDF1/HOTA result, or validated full-game player identity.

Three ambiguous development labels were changed to uncertain and one different-player label was corrected after raw-sequence review. Every candidate uses the same corrected development reference. Historical tables below retain their original references and should not be compared by subtracting their counts.

Tracker fine-tuning: six training runs and transfer tests

Result: the learned features improved Massachusetts validation but did not establish a consistent transfer gain. In the existing Great Britain clips, revised association with generic features recovered more true links than the fine-tuned features under the same rules. The existing default is retained; all variants remain available for inspection.

The detector is held fixed. OSNet appearance features were trained on California and New Jersey ball hockey: 130 reviewed identity pairs, plus up to 5,757 separately marked weak motion pairs. Massachusetts supplied 81 crop pairs for checkpoint selection and 85 tracking pairs for association selection. Great Britain supplied neither training nor selection data. Six adaptations tested a projection head, later backbone layers, weak-label augmentation, full-backbone adaptation and 512 learned channel weights.

The selected trained candidate is extended_full. Association settings were selected from 25 candidates per appearance model. The revised rule combines overlap and appearance, rejects contradictions in both matching stages, and makes duplicate suppression appearance-aware.

Excluding spectators

The player filter was trained on crops from three games and selected on Pittsburgh. On 12 selected Massachusetts frames, the displayed SAM2 variant matched 90 of 93 reviewed player observations. Eight reviewed nonplayers were still matched. Two unmatched outputs were not scored, so this is not exhaustive precision.

A separate learned rink-region model failed on Massachusetts, often selecting the spectator area. It is disabled in the displayed results. An assisted boundary-propagation method is being tested separately.

The frozen player filter also lost coverage on new venues. Applied after RF-DETR, it removed four of six Buffalo false detections but also removed 15 correct players, including six goalies. In Great Britain it removed two false detections and six correct players. This combination is not adopted as a general improvement. Transfer diagnostic.

Supplying several frames together to the 235B model gave 32 correct readings, three wrong readings and one abstention on the 36 readable cases. The single-image answers in a separate run gave 31 correct, one wrong and four abstentions. Combining those separate-image requests and requiring at least two agreeing readings with no conflict gave 25 correct, zero wrong and 11 abstentions. Requiring the original crop to agree reduced that to 23 correct and 13 abstentions.

The joint method added 26 readings without a central-image reference; the two separate-request rules added 15 and five. Those extra readings are unsupported, not necessarily false. Separate prompts still have correlated errors. Neighboring crops come from an automatic track and may belong to different players. These are development audits, not evidence that consensus solves identity. Separate-image results.

Estimating goals directly from video

Uniform scans of three complete recordings with an 8B video model found none of 24 reviewed goals and proposed two false events. A 235B model found two of 24 and proposed one false event. A newer 27B model at higher video resolution found eight of 24 reviewed goals, with 21 false events. Raising the 235B scan resolution across all three recordings found six of 24 goals, with three false events; it still found none of the seven Massachusetts goals. This is a development ablation. Events are matched once, within two seconds of reviewed intervals. Scoreboard review and official records remain more useful than these tested automatic goal scans.

Reading the scoreboard

A text-prompted board detector searched two full recordings every five seconds. On 58 previously readable New Jersey score references, the small reader got 56 right and two wrong. A separate 51-crop review exposed a more serious failure: on partial boards, it often read the period digit as a hidden guest score, producing 32 guest readings without visible support.

Repeated readings can repeat the same error. The automatic score-change timeline is not used for the official game statistics. Most Massachusetts board views were missing or cut off. A complete score is not inferred from a partial board. Exact-crop audit.

The 235B reader still supplied 31 guest scores without visible support on that same crop audit. A fixed development rule rejecting boards within 1% of the video frame edge removed all 51 difficult crops, including readable fields. With that rule, 235B gave 49 correct score pairs, zero wrong and nine abstentions on the 58 readable New Jersey references. No Massachusetts score state survived. This trades coverage for caution and is not an independent test. Large-reader crop audit · Edge-rejection results.

The guarded scoreboard pipeline produced eight New Jersey score changes matching the first eight official goals, but missed the final two. Its suggested review windows fully contained only six reviewed goal intervals; one touched a boundary and one fell outside because the board updated late. What remains unresolved

  • Jersey identity: the tested vision-language models still misread clear numbers and sometimes give numbers for unreadable crops. They are not used to silently assign identities.
  • Ball: the expanded training set improves recall, but false positives and poor visibility still prevent dependable shot or possession totals. Unknown visibility is not treated as a negative label.
  • Distance: the California venue is Irvine Inline Rink 3; the Massachusetts record names Grey Rink at Coolidge Park; New Jersey names Marge Martin Sports Complex. Rink-specific dimensions have not been verified. No miles or speeds are reported.
  • Time on floor: a camera-visible track fragment is not a full shift. Occlusion, substitutions and off-camera time remain unresolved.
  • Scoring: official goals and assists are identified as records. Reviewed video windows are separate. A scoreboard can update tens of seconds after the actual goal.
